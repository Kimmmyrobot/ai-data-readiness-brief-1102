A Decision-Led Data Readiness Brief for Business AI

Data is ready when it can support a defined decision under real operating conditions, not when a warehouse simply contains a large volume of records.

Readiness is contextual

Data readiness is often reduced to cleanliness, but a perfectly formatted dataset can still be unsuitable. It may describe the wrong population, reflect an outdated policy, omit the outcome the business cares about, or be unavailable for the intended use. Readiness is a relationship between information, a decision, and an operating responsibility.

The broader strategy at Coffee Cup AI becomes actionable when each proposed use is tied to a specific data contract. That contract states what the information means, where it came from, who may use it, how quickly it changes, and how the organization will detect when it stops representing reality.

01
  • Defined decision
  • Required evidence
  • Observable outcome

Start with the decision and its evidence

Write the decision or task in one sentence. Then list the facts a qualified person would need without AI. This prevents the project from treating available fields as a complete view of the problem. It also reveals important knowledge that lives in documents, conversations, or human experience rather than a central database.

Identify the outcome that can validate the use. A routing model needs evidence about whether the case reached the right destination. A forecast needs later actuals. A drafting tool needs a review record. Without an observable outcome, the team can measure activity but not usefulness.

02
  • Business meaning
  • System of record
  • Named data owner

Create shared definitions

Different systems may use the same label for different concepts. ‘Active customer’ might mean a current contract in finance, a recent login in product, or an open opportunity in sales. A model can combine those records while producing a result no department interprets consistently.

Build a small glossary for fields that influence the use case. Record the business meaning, source system, owner, update frequency, and known limitations. Resolve only the definitions needed for the pilot, but make disagreements visible rather than burying them in transformation code.

03
  • Representative coverage
  • Permitted purpose
  • Controlled access

Test representation, permission, and access

Examine whose activity appears in the data and whose does not. Historical records may overrepresent long-standing customers, successful transactions, or cases that were easy to classify. Missing groups and changed market conditions can make a technically strong model unreliable in current use.

Confirm that collection consent, contracts, privacy rules, and internal policy permit the intended processing. Apply least-privilege access and minimize copied data. A pilot should not create an uncontrolled shadow dataset simply because moving information into a prototype is convenient.

04
  • Monitor input change
  • Track reviewer overrides
  • Define pause conditions

Plan for drift before launch

Definitions, customer behavior, products, and workflows change. Choose signals that indicate the data contract is weakening, such as rising missing values, a shift in source mix, more reviewer overrides, or a drop in performance for a specific case type.

Assign someone to review those signals and define the response. The answer may be retraining, revising the workflow, narrowing the scope, or pausing the system. Data readiness is therefore not a gate passed once. It is an operating practice that continues for the life of the use.

Questions leaders ask

Does a business need perfect data before using AI?

No. It needs data that is sufficiently defined, representative, permitted, and monitored for a bounded use with known limitations.

Who should own data readiness?

Business and data owners should share responsibility, supported by technical, privacy, security, and risk specialists as the use requires.

What is a practical first readiness test?

Trace several real cases from source records to outcome and ask whether a reviewer can explain every material field and limitation.

The operating takeaway

Decision-led readiness keeps data work proportional. It directs attention to the definitions, permissions, evidence, and monitoring that determine whether an AI use can be trusted in practice.
